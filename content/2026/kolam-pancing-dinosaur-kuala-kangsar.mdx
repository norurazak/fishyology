#### Kolam Pancing Dinosaur, Kuala Kangsar Perak

It was early November, during Deepavali, when we had the opportunity to travel back to my wife's hometown for our nephew's wedding. The kids had just finished their two-week examination period, and I had just completed the main segment of the semester, so this short break was definitely most welcome, a much needed one, as we recharge to keep the momentum going into 2026. Now, about this wedding, it's crazy how time flies. It feels like only yesterday that I took him out fishing. Back then, it was at Thye Fishing Village. Still, to me, it's the best SW fishing pond to have ever existed in Malaysia. Period. Fast forward a few years (I don't even know how many!), and the guy is now ready to embark on his own journey in life. InSyaAllah, may both of you last forever, until Jannah.

Planning a fishing trip during a wedding getaway may not be the best idea, as timing is almost everything. But with the new monsoon season starting to kick in, it was raining across Alor Setar, Kedah. I wanted to take them somewhere with a more natural setting, perhaps fishing along Sungai Kedah. Unfortunately, due to the rain and timing, we couldn't lock in a fishing session, leaving us with just one more day, which was also the day we were heading back to KL. So, we decided to make a quick stop along the way for a short fishing session. Our best bet was the relatively new Kolam Pancing Dinosaur in Kuala Kangsar, Perak. A new fishing pond, I believe it was established around the middle of this year, and from what I've seen on social media, it is home to Chao Phraya catfish. Right after checking out, we stopped by Kuala Kedah for a quick lunch. The food was amazing, by the way, and from there, we set our next destination to Kuala Kangsar. Luckily, traffic was smooth. I guess people were still celebrating, and we had also decided to head back a day earlier.

The journey took us around 3 hours from Alor Setar. Kolam Pancing Dinosaur is situated close to the Kuala Kangsar exit, probably about 10 minutes from the highway exit. I had seen the photos beforehand, and my first impression was that it had the same vibe as the natural, exotic fishing ponds in Behrang or Rawang. But upon arriving, it really did feel like stepping into a lost valley from another time, especially with the whole aesthetic fitting its name. I know it's still a work in progress, but man, the pond was really nice. They have a dedicated front desk, and they sell most of the basic fishing tackle you would need at the pond, including fishing lines, hooks and sinkers. They also offer rod rentals at around RM50 per session, with each session lasting approximately four hours. The fishing rate is RM100, which is pretty standard compared to other "exotic fish fishing ponds". They also sell bait, including pellets and even live bait such as catfish and silver barb.

They have dedicated fishing hut, to make it abit more private. Those dedicated hut would have a deciated station, table, power points, fan and rod holder. Pretty neat! And my family can now be stationed on that hut. We parked and get all the stuff ready. We are going to go with cut keli for bait this time around. I know it’s a pretty basic approach and one special bait to be tested here today. The setup that we use, we had both Abu Garcia lineup ready. Abu Garcia Cardinal III 5000 size reel loaded with SeaHunter 20lb line and Abu Garcia Revo Rods, 17-40lb rating should be able to do the job fine here. Although the reel does feel a touch light, without the upgraded carbon drags but we will go with what we have. We opted for the Chunk Bait Bottom Rig, a small sinker to hold the bait station and a 7/0 J hook, debarb to do all the hooking stuff. On chunk of bait at a time. The trick here is to ensure the hook is exposed to be able to set the hook properly.

We can clearly a school of Chao Phraya Catfish was swimming close to our hut. We drop in our first cast and was on to my second rod to setup. I think less than 5 minutes, the first bite of the day registered. Aarif set the hook properly and off he go to fight the first fish of the day. The fish is showcasing the usual trait of a Chao Phraya Catfish, is that constant back and forth of reeling in it close and as soon as it reaches 5-10 meters it would swim off again. The session provide a great training for Aarif and Hana to practice the right tempo or ryhtm of reeling big fish. After about 10 minutes, Aarif finally landed his Chao Phraya Catfish without any assistance haha, it was a small size, lesser than his personal best in Fish Valley, Semenyih.

I knew the bite was going to be quick and next would be Hana. She seems a bit intimidated by the size of the fish at first. Quickly rigged another one and cast out hoping to get a second bite. Again doesn’t take long before another Chao Phraya Catfish hammers the bait. This time was definitely bigger than last time as I can feel it at the end of the line when I set the hook. Pass it over to Hana and being competitive in nature, she would take on her biggest fish of her life. She seems to struggle a bit earlier as the fish was really not giving slack and the drag on the Abu Garcia Cardinal III just barely hang on to it. As soon as she figure out that uses the rod to pull the fish and reel in as fast as possible to reporsition to rod for the next full, she soon start to reel in line. This time took about 15 minutes, was a bit too worried the fish could be in danger after prolong fight but the fish hold on nicely.

The next round took a little longer before we finally got another bite. We were still using cut catfish, around 2-3 inches in length. They sell them here at RM4.00 per fish, although I think you can get them cheaper elsewhere. The blood and aroma from the catfish (ikan keli) are definitely the main triggers that got these Chao Phraya Catfish biting. I didn't want to go for numbers today, so I decided to pace things a little and give the kids some breathing room to catch up, haha. After all, 10-15 minutes of fighting a fish can be pretty taxing on your strength. I told Aarif to reel in and check the bait, and BAM! The fish took it while he was retrieving the line, haha. A few minutes later, the second rod went off, and we were on a double hookup this time! I was a little more confident now about letting them fight the fish on their own, and soon enough, Aarif landed our third Chao Phraya Catfish while Hana landed another massive one. So far, so good at Kolam Pancing Dinosaur!

We continued fishing right until the end of the day, wrapping things up with a total of 10 Chao Phraya Catfish landed and another 2 lost along the way. Not a bad result at all, considering we were only there for a short session! One thing worth mentioning is the SeaHunter Fight PEX8 fishing line. It's a well-built fishing line from China, purchased through TikTok Shop, and it has performed remarkably well throughout the session. Despite the constant pressure and powerful runs from these fish, the line held up nicely even on medium drag settings. And remember, we're talking about a 20lb-rated line here. Amazing! It's always satisfying when your tackle performs beyond expectations, especially when you're pushing it against fish of this size. The owner was also really happy to see two kids battling it out with these massive Chao Phraya Catfish, and we even made it onto their live show, haha! That was a nice little bonus to wrap up the day. Thank you, boss, for the hospitality and for making this a memorable fishing session for the family.

The final fight was again on double hookup, I think today Hana never hooked up the smaller size Chao Phraya Catfish, and were happy to see finally Aarif breaking his personal best with his massive Chao at the end. Overall, Kolam Pancing Dinosaur was a really pleasant surprise and definitely a place worth visiting if you're into exotic freshwater fishing. Despite still being a work in progress, the pond has plenty of potential, and the facilities are already pretty decent. The dedicated fishing huts, friendly owner, readily available bait and tackle, and most importantly, the quality of fish make it a worthwhile stop. What I appreciate most is that it is also family-friendly, allowing the kids to enjoy the experience without feeling out of place. Watching them take on fish of this size, learning to manage the rod, work the drag and eventually land their own catches was probably the highlight of the entire trip. We came here hoping for a short fishing session on our way back to KL, but ended up with 10 Chao Phraya Catfish landed and plenty of memories to take home. Will definitely keep this place on our list for another visit. If you're ever travelling along the North-South Expressway and happen to pass Kuala Kangsar, this might just be a worthwhile detour. Until the next fishing adventure!
