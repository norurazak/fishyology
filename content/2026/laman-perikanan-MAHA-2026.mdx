## Malaysia's Biggest Agriculture Show
#### MAHA 2026: Inside Laman Perikanan and the Future of Malaysia's Fisheries Industry

Laman Perikanan, situated in District 2 was part of a bigger showcase of the Malaysia Agriculture, Horticulture & Agrotourism (MAHA) 2026, marked (Yellow – District 2) on the event map alongside other amazing agricultural showcase and the state pavilions. That single line was enough to draw me in to understand a bit more on what is happening withing the industry and community. Malaysia's fisheries industry rarely gets a dedicated stage in front of a crowd of this scale, and when it does, MAHA 2026 offers a great opportunity for visitors, investors and market players to see the industry up close, discover emerging technologies and solutions, and explore opportunities across the fisheries and aquaculture value chain.

Fortunately, the extra 1-day holiday on 1 Sep 2026 (Selangor winning SUKMA 2026) provide me and my family with the opportunity to visit MAHA 2026. The expo runs from 28 August until 6 September 2026 at MAEPS Serdang, Selangor. This year, MAHA 2026 bringing together more than 2,000 exhibitors across 42,000 square metres of indoor exhibition space and 130 hectares outdoors, with the total number of visitors are expected to reach 2.5 million by end of the 10-day event.

After investigating a bit more of the event, the first expo ran from 30 June to 2 July 1923 on the Selangor Turf Club field, and the 2024 edition, opened by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim at MAEPS, was marked as its 100th anniversary. It has been biennial and Serdang-based for years now, big enough that the Malaysia Book of Records named it the largest exhibition in Southeast Asia in 2008 and large enough since to pull roughly three million visitors an edition. What has changed is the framing around those crowds. MAHA 2016 ran under "Our Food, Our Future"; 2022 under "Food Security for the Future"; and 2026 under Penciptaan Nilai untuk Keterjaminan Makanan. Three consecutive editions, making essentially the same case with escalating urgency.

#### MAHA 2026: The States Pavilion

From Laman Tiba 1, we took a buggy straight to District 2 and it dropped us off right at the main entrance to the states pavilion. We started off with the Sarawak Pavilion; there has been a lot of praises on threads that this is the best pavilion. It showcases Sarawakian Micro, Small and Medium Enterprise selling Agri based produces such as peppercorns and Kuih Lapis. It also showcases traditional attire and dances, truly putting in a great effort to highlight the best that Sarawak has to offer.

For me, the Perak Pavilion was the most beautiful of all. The pavilion beautifully showcases the unique architectural elements of traditional Perak houses, giving visitors a glimpse into the state's rich heritage and distinctive identity. The exhibitor showcases a full display of the Perak State Government initiative in Agri and Fisheries based industry. The Fisheries industry has been an integral contributor to the state economy, given the state's extensive coastline, rivers, and rich aquatic resources.

Perak coastal line has been and important fishing ground for recreational saltwater anglers. Fishing spot such as Pulau Sembilan and Pulau Jarak offers a variety of SW game fish species. Inland Perak is also known for its freshwater recreational fishing post such as Royal Belum and Lenggong. These location serves an important contributor for local fishing economy through localised tourism and it's good to see the Perak Fisheries Department is doing their best to ensure the sustainability of these ecosystems.

#### MAHA 2026: Fishing with The Milkfish Lady

One session that we all look forward to was to experience fishing in MAHA 2026. Shamin and Naweshad co-founded The Milkfish Lady, a Malaysian fishing and marine education initiative that has been taking families fishing since 2014. The organisation focuses on education, sustainability, conservation, and ethical fishing, introducing both children and adults to recreational fishing while sharing knowledge about aquatic life and responsible fishing practices. With over 4000 kids, hands down to this guys on educating the community

During MAHA 2026, they are offering basic fishing experience and offer simple fishing equipment and guidance. They provide useful walkthrough such as fishing knot, fish measurement and dehooking a fish, making the experience accessible to all beginners. On top of that you get yourself a free online IGFA membership and a certificate of participation. If you are an experienced angler, you can come to the spot with your own gear and bait. I've seen amazing catch report through MAHA 2026 on social media and at the same time, they are clearing the invasive species from the pond. Peacock bass are being transported to a different location.

#### MAHA 2026: Laman Perikanan Malaysia

Our final stop was at the Perikanan Centre, where we explored an exhibition focusing on biosecurity and food security, as well as Malaysia's native freshwater fish species. I found the biosecurity exhibition particularly interesting, especially as an educational activity for children. Hana and Aarif walk through the different stages of the process; from sampling and examining fish specimens to reviewing the samples under a microscope. In many ways, these activities provide a glimpse into the kind of work carried out by fisheries auditors and inspectors to ensure that Malaysia's aquatic resources and food supply remain safe.

Biosecurity is an increasingly important part of food safety, particularly for Malaysia, which imports a significant number of fish and other aquatic produce from neighbouring countries such as Thailand. Proper inspection, monitoring and testing help minimise the risk of diseases and harmful organisms being introduced through imported aquatic products. It was a fun experience for Hana and Aarif to be able to experience the process, and it was presented in a way that was accessible, fun and engaging.

#### MAHA 2026: The Overall Outlook

Overall, MAHA 2026 was an amazing experience and, for me and my family, a very informative one. The exhibitions offered plenty of knowledge and insight into Malaysia's agriculture, fisheries, food security, biodiversity and related industries. Although for us we only spend time partially within District 2, the experiential activities there are meaningful.

What I appreciated most was that MAHA was not simply about showcasing products and technology. It also provided an opportunity to educate the public, introduce our local industries, highlight our natural resources and give visitors a better understanding of the work that goes on behind the scenes to keep these sectors moving forward.

I hope similar programmes will continue to be organised in the future, hopefully, become even bigger and better. Most importantly, I hope these initiatives will continue to inspire and educate our younger generations, so that they can better appreciate Malaysia's agricultural and aquatic heritage and play their part in protecting it for generations to come.
